Training NCAs:
what helped, and what didn't

Jonas Mehtali · University of Strasbourg / ICube
All the numbers in this talk come from the model you will train in the hands-on: a CT slice and a needle go in, the burn and the vessels come out. The reference model has 46,240 parameters.

Why an NCA is harder to train than a CNN

CNN
depth 12, each layer its own weights, one pass
NCA
one small rule, applied 10 to 40 times

What you will see when it goes wrong:

  • loss goes to NaN after a few hundred steps
  • loss goes down, output is flat zero
  • perfect at 12 steps, garbage at 40
  • fine while training, drifts when you keep iterating

One small set of weights, applied over and over, with no normalisation and no view of the whole image. Most of what follows comes back to that.

Two fixes that cost two lines each

Zero-init the last convolution

nn.init.zeros_(block[-1].weight)

p = model(x, steps=20)          # untrained
assert float(p.max() - p.min()) == 0.0

Before training, the rule does nothing, so the model starts from a safe place. If the output is not flat, your state layout is wrong, and you find out in two seconds instead of after an hour of training.

Normalise gradients per parameter

loss.backward()
for p in model.parameters():
    p.grad /= p.grad.norm() + 1e-8
clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), 1.0)
opt.step()

After a long rollout, some layers get gradients orders of magnitude larger than others, and it changes from batch to batch. Clipping the global norm does not fix that imbalance.

Sample the rollout length, then teach it to stay

k = randint(3, 10)        # every batch
pred, state = model(x, k)
loss = L(pred, y)

# sometimes: keep going
state = model(x, 150, state).detach()
loss += L(model(x, 6, state), y)

Train with a fixed K and the model learns a routine that ends at step K. Vary K and it has to settle into a stable state. Now and then, let it run much longer and check it again, so it learns to stay there.

DSC at 400 steps: 0.672 → 0.840

Keep the environment out of the model's reach

necrosisvessels CTneedlepower
answersinputs, restored every stepscratch
for _ in range(steps):
    x = step(x)
    x = cat([x[:, :2], inputs, x[:, 5:]], 1)

If the model can overwrite its inputs, it will. Our 3-D model did not restore them, and the power channel ended up at 7 630× its normal value, the needle at 75×. The plan was basically gone.

Writing them back every step is one line, and it is what lets you move a needle mid-rollout and watch the burn follow.

Scratch channels are where the model computes

Two channels hold the answers and three hold the inputs. A 6-channel model has just one channel left to think with.

Add width and both outputs improve together. The two tasks were fighting over memory, not over the gradient, and no loss weighting gives you a spare channel.

Same training budget for each, real CT, before the loss fix on the next slide.

Check the share, not the weight

loss = necrosis_loss + w * vessel_loss

I set w = 3.0 while fighting a stuck vessel output and never went back to it. The necrosis output ended up with 3 % of the loss.

val DSC at epoch 10
0.46 → 0.79

Same data, same budget, only that number changed. Whenever you change a loss, print how much each term contributes.

Two knobs that are free after training

Rollout length K

Choose it on validation: the smallest K within 1 % of the best (dashed line).

Decision threshold

Our search stopped at 0.7, but the best value was higher. That is +0.027 vessel F1 without retraining.

Things that did not work for us

Rotations and flips looked free
vessel F1 0.699 → 0.280

The heat equation does not care about orientation, but axial CT does: the liver is always on the right.

"bf16 supported" on a T4-class GPU
33× slower per batch

It is emulated on older cards. Check the compute capability instead of trusting is_bf16_supported().

A random fire mask
vessel F1 0.674 → 0.715 without it

The classic Growing-NCA trick. Here, updating every cell at every step trained better, and the result is deterministic.

A vessel-only warm start
learned vessels ½ as fast

Compared with training both outputs from the start. Learning the burn seems to help it find the vessels.

Change one thing at a time, and measure a trick before you keep it.

Freeze what the plan must not touch

The vessels do not depend on the needle. But a model that sees the plan while looking for them learns a shortcut: inside a burn, there are no vessels.

So we run a few steps on the CT alone, then switch the needle on and freeze the vessel channel, just like the CT. Same rule, same weights.

vessel F1 with random plans
0.691 → 0.769

Measured with the notebook's 5-minute recipe, 2 to 3 seeds, test plans.
